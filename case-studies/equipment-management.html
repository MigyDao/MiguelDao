Equipment Management System

Turning tools, maintenance events, parts needs and project work into one maintainable operating system.

Case snapshot

Role
System design & implementation

Platform
Notion

Focus
Equipment · maintenance · parts · projects

Status
In use and evolving

Challenge

Physical equipment creates information that is easy to scatter.

Condition, repairs, consumables, replacement parts and project use can end up split across memory, notes and one-off task lists. That makes it harder to answer basic operating questions: What condition is this tool in? What failed last time? Which part does it need? Is the repair already part of a project?

I designed the system around a central equipment record rather than treating maintenance as a disconnected checklist.

Architecture

Keep identity separate from events and needs.

  • Equipment Tracker: the durable identity, condition and current-state record.
  • Maintenance & Repairs: historical faults, service actions and repair work.
  • Parts & Consumables: replacement needs, recurring consumables and sourcing context.
  • Projects: larger work that uses, repairs or improves equipment.

This keeps the current state readable without erasing the history that explains how it got there.

Design principle

Support repairability and long-term capability.

The system is not just an inventory count. It is meant to make repair, maintenance and replacement decisions easier to reason about over time. The same architecture also makes it possible to connect physical workshop activity with planning and project management rather than keeping those worlds separate.

Outcome

A clearer source of truth for maintenance decisions.

The system centralizes equipment condition, repair history and parts planning into one maintainable structure. It demonstrates relational system design and practical maintenance thinking while making recurring maintenance decisions easier to trace.
